## Cross-Study Patient Mean at Inference

The supplement's Section 7(a) explicitly classifies a fixed, deterministic aggregation of model predictions for multiple test images from the same patient, such as a mean or majority vote, as allowed post-processing when it fits no parameters, uses no test labels, and uses no test-set statistics. It says this is consistent with the rule's express permission for fixed TTA and model ensembles. The wording says multiple images belonging to the same patient; it does not limit the images to one Study. Therefore a fixed mean across Studies belonging to the same patient is within the stated permission, provided the patient grouping comes from official data structure or metadata and the operation is fixed and deterministic.

For a Study-level task, the least ambiguous fixed formula is: first aggregate image predictions to each Study under the existing view protocol, then take an equal-weight mean of those Study predictions within each patient. This avoids implicitly giving a Study more weight merely because it contains more images. The formula, official metadata field used for grouping, and order of aggregation should be disclosed. It must not be adjusted using test outcomes.

There is a textual tension in Section 7(c): after allowing the operation in 7(a), it says not to use test labels “or any test-set information.” Read literally, that phrase would also prohibit the test predictions and official patient metadata that 7(a) necessarily requires. The specific permission in 7(a) and its explicit metadata instruction are best read as permitting only those necessary inputs for the fixed aggregation, while preserving the bans on labels, fitted parameters, test-derived statistics, and any adaptive adjustment. This is an interpretation of the inconsistent wording, not a claim that the tension is absent. A submission should state the exact inputs and formula rather than claim broad permission to use test information.

Permission is not evidence of benefit. The previously reported res768 E3 validation comparison of patient means had Macro-AUC delta `-0.030285`, with patient-bootstrap 95% CI `[-0.050753, -0.011153]`; class 1 delta was `-0.023204` and class 4 delta was `-0.018880`. These are exploratory, not preregistered, and this review did not recompute them. They argue against adopting cross-Study smoothing on current evidence, even though a fixed implementation appears permitted by the specific supplement clause.

The subsequent [E1 recheck](../../resolution_e1_analysis_20261103/analysis/patient_mean_e1.md) was registered before its calculation but explicitly after seeing that E3 negative result, so it is a non-independent exploratory recheck. On stored official-valid E1 hflip2 predictions, the fixed patient mean lowered Macro-AUC from `0.807556` to `0.777570` (delta `-0.029986`, 2000 paired patient-bootstrap 95% CI `[-0.050144, -0.012057]`); AP-only delta was `-0.033592`, CI `[-0.059655, -0.013044]`. Class 1 and class 4 point deltas were negative but their individual intervals crossed zero. The complete ten-class results, exact inputs, replay script and receipt accompany that report. This supports rejecting the unconditional mean on current validation evidence; it does not establish that every train-only patient-relation method must fail.

## Patient-Consistency Loss Using Official Train Only

Article 2 permits supervised fine-tuning using official training data. Article 4 expressly permits training losses such as class weighting and other augmentation that introduces no test information. The rules do not name a patient-consistency loss specifically, but contain no explicit prohibition on an auxiliary relation loss formed solely from official train labels and official train patient/Study grouping. On the written text, it is permissible if all relation targets and groups come from official train only; it does not use validation or test predictions, labels, grouping statistics, or fitted values; and it is documented as part of the training method.

The current experiment constraint is stricter than the general rule: validation may be used only for early stopping and comparison. Therefore validation must not contribute examples, patient relationships, targets, loss terms, or fitted consistency strength. The loss and coefficient should be fixed from the train-only design and preregistered before any run. This establishes a compliance boundary, not a recommendation that the loss will improve AUC. In particular, permission to use train patient relationships does not imply that different-time Studies should have equal labels or probabilities. Longitudinal Studies may legitimately differ, so an unconditional same-patient equality loss is not recommended; it can erase real changes. A future train-only relation design would need to preserve each Study's supplied labels rather than replace them with patient-level targets.

## Scoring Unit and Practical Implication

The submitted format is Study × class, and the repository specification says images are aggregated to Study before emitting the ten class probabilities. Together with the recorded official-answer description that labels are check/Study-level, this supports evaluating predictions at Study level. The rule text itself does not separately state a scoring-key field, so this conclusion is grounded in the official-answer record and submission contract rather than a quoted scoring clause.

A patient mean across Studies gives the same prediction to all Studies for a patient and can collapse between-Study ranking differences into ties. That can lower Study-level AUC even when the post-processing is permitted. The current reported validation result is negative and its confidence interval excludes zero, but it remains exploratory because the comparison was not preregistered.
